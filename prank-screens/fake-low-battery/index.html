This free fake low battery screen makes a phone look like it is minutes from dying. Launch it and a lock screen fills the display with a battery percentage that genuinely counts down, second by second. Low-battery warnings appear at 20%, 10% and 5% exactly as the real system shows them, and every button on those warnings makes things worse rather than better. At 0% the screen goes black and the empty-battery icon appears, just like a phone that has actually died. Your real battery is never touched, and ESC ends it instantly.

What the fake low battery prank does

Most “dead phone” jokes are a static picture, and static pictures do not survive being looked at for more than a few seconds. This one is live. The battery figure in the status bar drops in real time, the icon empties as it goes, and the colour shifts from white to amber at 20% and to red at 10% — the same thresholds the phone itself uses. By the time the warnings start stacking up, the person holding the phone has already watched the number fall with their own eyes, which is what makes a fake dead phone convincing where a screenshot never is.

iPhone or Android

  • iPhone — iOS lock screen. The thin oversized clock, the date beneath it, and the centred iOS alert reading “Low Battery — 10% battery remaining” with the Low Power Mode and OK buttons side by side.
  • Android — lock screen. A cooler wallpaper, Roboto type, and the dark rounded Material dialog reading “Battery low — 10% remaining. Turn on Battery Saver to extend battery life.” with No thanks and Turn on.

Match the style to the phone you are pranking. Handing an iPhone user a screen full of Material dialogs gives the whole thing away in about a second, and the reverse is just as obvious.

How to run the fake low battery screen

  1. Pick the phone style — iPhone or Android.
  2. Choose a starting battery level. 25% is a slow burn, 15% is the natural middle, and 5% starts in crisis with a warning almost immediately.
  3. Set the drain speed. Slow takes about three minutes to reach zero, Normal about a minute, Fast about twenty seconds.
  4. Leave the warnings on unless you want a silent slide to zero with no pop-ups at all.
  5. Launch it fullscreen and hand the phone over. Press ESC, or close the tab, to end it.

Every button makes it worse

This is the part that turns a countdown into a prank. When the low-battery warning appears, the natural reaction is to dismiss it — or to tap Low Power Mode or Turn on Battery Saver, because that is what those buttons are for. Here, every one of them costs another two percent immediately and brings the next warning forward.

Nobody notices the first time. By the third warning, the person holding the phone has worked out that tapping is making it drain faster, and they stop touching it entirely — which is the funniest moment the fake low battery prank produces, because by then the number is in single figures and there is nothing left to try.

What happens at 20%, 10% and 5%

The fake low battery follows the same three-stage script a real phone uses, because those thresholds are what people have been trained by their own devices to react to:

  • 20% — the first warning. The status bar turns amber and the first alert appears. Most people glance at it, dismiss it, and carry on, which is exactly what you want: they have now seen the number and accepted it.
  • 10% — the one that lands. The battery icon turns red and the second warning arrives noticeably sooner than expected, because dismissing the first one cost them charge. This is usually the point where somebody says “your battery is about to die”.
  • 5% — no way back. The final warning, a single-figure percentage, and a countdown that is now visibly moving. Nothing they tap helps, and the fake low battery is only seconds from the black screen.

Starting the prank below one of those levels simply skips that warning — begin at 5% and the first alert lands almost immediately, which is the right choice if you only have someone’s attention for a few seconds rather than a few minutes.

The dead phone screen

At the end of the fake low battery countdown the lock screen fades to black and the empty-battery icon appears in the middle — the outline with the red sliver that a phone shows when there is not enough charge left to boot. After a couple of seconds it fades out and the screen is simply black.

Tapping wakes it exactly like a real dead phone prank victim would expect: the icon flashes up again for a moment, then disappears. That single detail is what sells it. A phone that stays black no matter what looks broken; a phone that briefly shows the empty-battery icon each time you press something looks flat, and flat is a problem people believe in immediately.

Hand the phone over: a worked example

  1. Set it up on your own phone at 15% with a Slow drain, matching your phone’s style.
  2. Launch it, then hand the phone over with a reason — a photo to look at, a number to type in, a link to read.
  3. Say nothing. They will notice the battery themselves, and mention it before you do.
  4. Let them tap the warning and watch the number drop further. Most people apologise at this point, which is the whole reward.
  5. Let it die in their hands, then take the phone back and press ESC — the real percentage reappears and the fake low battery is over.

Why a browser-based fake low battery beats an app

Search for a fake low battery screen and almost everything you find is an app: a download, a permissions prompt, adverts, and something left installed on the phone afterwards. Installing an app to run a ten-second joke is a poor trade, and asking to borrow someone’s phone to install something is not a promising start to a prank.

This is a web page. It opens instantly, installs nothing, asks for no permissions, and leaves nothing behind when the tab closes. It also cannot read your real battery level — a page like this has no reason to ask for that, and it does not.

Is the fake low battery prank safe?

Completely. The percentage on screen is a number counting down inside the page, with no connection whatsoever to the actual battery. Nothing is drained, no setting is changed, no app is installed, and the phone cannot be switched off by a web page. The moment you press ESC or close the tab, the real status bar reappears showing the true charge, which is usually the fastest way to prove the joke.

Getting it fullscreen on a phone

On Android, tapping launch hides Chrome’s toolbar and the illusion is close to perfect. On an iPhone the situation is more awkward and worth knowing before you try: Safari does not let a web page take over the entire screen, so a slim browser bar stays visible at the edge. The fix is to add this page to your Home Screen first and open it from that icon — it then launches without the browser interface, and the fake low battery screen fills the display properly.

If you cannot be bothered with that, hold the phone at a slight angle or hand it over already launched. People look at the enormous clock and the battery figure, not at the two millimetres of browser chrome above them.

Fake low battery vs. a genuinely dying phone

A real phone at 1% dims the screen, throttles performance, and eventually shuts down properly — after which the power button produces the charging icon and nothing else until you plug it in. This prank imitates the visible half of that and none of the rest: the phone stays fully awake behind a black page, every app is still running, and no charge has been lost.

The tell, if anyone works it out, is that the phone never actually powers down — the screen simply shows black. That is also the reassurance to offer afterwards, along with the true battery percentage sitting in the status bar the second you exit.

Tips for a believable dead phone prank

  • Start higher than you think. 15% with a slow drain is far more convincing than starting at 5% — the fall is the story, not the number.
  • Match the phone style to the actual device, including which side of the dialog the buttons sit on.
  • Give them a reason to hold it — a photo, a text to read. A phone handed over for no reason invites suspicion.
  • Do not narrate. Pointing out the battery is the one thing that will make them doubt it.
  • Let them reach the dead screen. The black screen with the empty-battery icon is the payoff; ending early wastes it.

Prank responsibly

As pranks go this is one of the gentler ones — nobody thinks their data is gone, only their charge. It still lands badly in the wrong moment. Do not use a fake low battery on someone waiting for a call from a hospital or a job, on anyone travelling alone or relying on maps to get home, or on a person who is already anxious about being unreachable. Stay nearby, keep it short, and show them ESC while it is still funny.
