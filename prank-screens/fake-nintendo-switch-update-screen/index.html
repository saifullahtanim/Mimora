This free fake Nintendo Switch update screen recreates the original Switch System Update interface in your browser. An “Updating…” dialog and a small spinner sit over the dimmed settings page. The animation finishes with the update-complete message and a black restart screen, then repeats. No console is contacted and nothing is installed.

What the fake Nintendo Switch update screen looks like

The background shows “System Update” at the top, an available-update message, the Update button and the controller hints along the bottom. During installation, the background dims and a centered dialog shows “Updating…” with a rotating spinner underneath. It does not display a percentage inside a large progress ring.

The fullscreen view and the card preview use the same layout. On a monitor or television, the dialog, heading and footer scale together. On a phone, spacing adjusts to keep the text and controls readable. Choose Basic Black for the dark console theme or Basic White for the light theme; the preview changes as you select it.

The complete update sequence

To see the interactive beginning, choose From the Update prompt before launching. The screen opens on “A system update is available. Update now?” Select Update to open the restart confirmation. It explains that the console will restart after the update and that suspended software will be closed. Select OK to continue, or Back to return to the Update page.

Next comes “Please wait…”, followed by “Updating…”. Both use the small spinner. The completion dialog reads “Update complete. The system will restart momentarily.” The screen then goes black with a spinner before repeating from Please wait. This repeated cycle is part of the browser simulation; it does not take you to a real console menu.

The fake Nintendo Switch update follows a normal update sequence. It does not insert a network error or claim that the console has failed. The screen timing is adjustable and simulated, so it should not be used to estimate how long a real Switch update will take.

Why a spinner is worse than a progress bar

The Switch installer shows no percentage and no progress bar at this stage, only “Updating…” and a small spinner. That is a gift for a prank. With a percentage, someone can at least estimate how long is left. With a spinner, they cannot tell whether the update is nearly finished or has barely started, so every extra second feels longer than the last.

Switch updates are also usually short. Owners of other consoles are used to sitting through long installs, but a Switch owner’s experience is that the update takes a moment and then it is done. So a fake Nintendo Switch update that keeps spinning becomes worrying quickly, and the joke needs far less patience from you to land than the same prank on another console.

Choose a starting screen

Updating is the default. It opens directly on the installation dialog, so the display looks as though an update is already underway. Choose Please wait to include the short stage before installation. Choose From the Update prompt when you want the initial Update button and restart confirmation.

The starting screen applies each time you launch the prank. After the first restart, later cycles of the fake Nintendo Switch update continue automatically from Please wait. You do not need to confirm the update every time it loops. To choose a different starting screen, exit the animation and change the setting on the page.

Adjust the speed and console theme

A full automatic cycle takes about three minutes on Slow, one minute on Normal, or 25 seconds on Fast. Opening directly on Updating skips the initial wait, making the first cycle a little shorter. If you start on the Update prompt, the animation waits for you to confirm before the timed sequence begins.

Basic Black uses a charcoal dialog over a dimmed dark settings page. Basic White uses a light dialog with dark text. The theme selection affects both the preview and fullscreen animation. The black restart screen is the same for either theme.

Match the theme to the console in the room. Regular players know which theme their Switch uses, and a dark fake Nintendo Switch update on a console they keep in Basic White is exactly the kind of detail they notice. If you are not sure, check the theme under System Settings on the console before you set up the prank.

How to launch the fake Nintendo Switch update

Select your starting screen, update speed and console theme, then press Start Fake Update. The fullscreen icon on the preview launches the same animation. For a television, open the page on a computer connected to that display and launch it there. You can also use a laptop, tablet or phone browser.

The console itself is not involved. You are displaying a browser recreation on the chosen screen. There is no need to sign in to a Nintendo Account, connect a controller, change the console settings or download an update file.

Where to run it, and an honest limitation

The Xbox version of this prank can be loaded in the console’s own browser. The Switch has no general-purpose web browser you can simply open, so the fake Nintendo Switch update has to come from another device. That leaves the television. With the console docked, connect a laptop to the same TV over HDMI, launch the prank, and then switch the TV to that input. The room sees the Switch sitting in its dock and a television showing its update screen, and nothing in that picture contradicts the story.

In handheld mode there is no honest way to do this, and it is better to know that up front. The screen is built into the console, so there is nothing to substitute. The same applies to the Switch Lite, which cannot output video to a TV at all. If the person you are pranking plays mostly handheld, pick a different prank rather than forcing this one.

A worked example

Here is a setup that works well. Pick the same theme the console uses. Leave the starting screen on Updating, so the display looks as though the install is already underway, and choose Slow if you are leaving the room or Normal if you want to watch. Launch the prank on the laptop, switch the TV input, and hand over the controller.

Opening on Updating is the strongest choice for most people, because it skips straight to the part that causes the worry. From the Update prompt suits a different joke, where you act out accepting the update yourself. The Update button and restart confirmation respond to the mouse and keyboard, not to the console’s controller. Either way, the fake Nintendo Switch update ends each cycle with “Update complete”, a black restart screen and then “Please wait…” all over again.

Buttons and keyboard controls

On the initial Update page, click Update or the A/OK control to open the confirmation. In the confirmation, Back returns to the previous screen and OK begins the animation. You can also use A to continue, B or Backspace to go back, and the arrow keys to move between the confirmation buttons. Enter activates the focused button.

During Please wait, Updating and completion, the dimmed background controls are inactive, matching the update dialog. The browser exit controls remain available throughout. There is no simulated button-press error or extra warning covering the update screen.

How to exit

Press ESC at any point to leave the fake Nintendo Switch update and return to the page. You can also click or tap the invisible exit area in the top-right corner. Moving the mouse does not show an exit badge. Closing the browser tab also ends the animation. Back on the initial Update page exits the simulation; Back in the confirmation returns to the Update prompt.

Exiting stops the update timer. The page does not continue into another stage or reopen the overlay afterward. Launching again starts a fresh cycle using your selected settings.

How long should you let it run?

Shorter than you think. One full cycle is usually the sweet spot: “Updating…” spins, the completion message appears, the screen goes black, and “Please wait…” begins again. On Normal that takes about a minute. The moment the fake Nintendo Switch update comes back from its restart and starts over is the punchline, so show them ESC soon after it lands.

Let it loop twice and the joke stops being a surprise and starts being someone’s actual evening.

When to leave this one alone

It is one of the gentler pranks on the site. Nobody believes their console is broken, only that it has picked the worst possible moment to update, which every player has lived through. That shared experience is why it works, and also why it is worth a little judgement about timing.

Do not run a fake Nintendo Switch update on someone about to start an online match with friends waiting, or a session they have been looking forward to all week. The joke is the sinking feeling of a badly timed update, and that feeling stops being funny if the match was real.
